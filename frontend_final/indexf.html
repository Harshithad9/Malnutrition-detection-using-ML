    Understanding Malnutrition

    What is Malnutrition?

    Malnutrition is a condition that develops when the body does not get enough nutrients to function properly. In children, early malnutrition can lead to impaired growth, cognitive development issues, and weakened immunity. Early detection and intervention are critical for optimal child development.

    Types of Malnutrition

    1. Wasting Acute

    Characterized by rapid weight loss and low weight-for-height ratio. This is the most visible form of acute malnutrition and requires immediate intervention.

    2. Stunting Chronic

    Results from prolonged inadequate nutrition, causing reduced height-for-age. This reflects chronic malnutrition and impacts long-term development.

    3. Underweight Mixed

    Indicates low weight-for-age, which can result from either acute or chronic malnutrition or both. This is a general indicator of poor nutritional status.

    4. Micronutrient Deficiencies Hidden

    Deficiencies in vitamins and minerals (iron, iodine, Vitamin A, etc.) that may not always be visible but severely impact immune function and development.

    Risk Factors

    Common risk factors include inadequate dietary intake, poor feeding practices, infections, unsanitary living conditions, maternal malnutrition, and limited access to healthcare services.

    Health Consequences

    Malnutrition weakens immune function, increases vulnerability to infections, impairs cognitive development, reduces growth potential, and can have lifelong impacts on physical and mental health.

    Why Early Detection Matters

    Early identification of malnutrition signs allows for timely nutritional interventions, improved treatment outcomes, and prevention of serious complications. NutriScan helps healthcare workers rapidly screen for potential malnutrition using both visual and anthropometric assessments.
